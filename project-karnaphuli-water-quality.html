Reading river pollution from space.

An investigation of water quality in Bangladesh's Karnaphuli River estuary, bringing together laboratory measurements, Sentinel-2 imagery, regression models and spatial pollution maps.

The methodology

Linking field evidence to satellite signals.

The study tested whether multispectral reflectance could help describe water-quality variation in a complex, industrial estuary.

01 / FIELD & LAB

Measure water quality

Fifty surface-water samples were tested for physicochemical indicators and heavy metals, providing observed values at known locations.

02 / EARTH OBSERVATION

Extract reflectance

Sentinel-2 surface reflectance was acquired through Google Earth Engine and processed alongside the sample locations.

03 / SPATIAL MODELLING

Compare patterns

Multiple linear regression and principal component regression were evaluated; GIS maps compared sampled-data interpolation with model-derived estimates.

Spatial outputs

Two views of the same river.

For selected parameters, the researchers compared surfaces interpolated from field observations with maps calculated from satellite-based regression models. The differences help show where satellite proxies remain uncertain.

Physicochemical indicator

Total suspended solids (TSS)

Reflectance explained the largest share of modelled variation in the study (reported R² = 0.58).

Heavy-metal indicator

Aluminium (Al)

Maps explored spatial differences in metal concentrations, using satellite reflectance only as an indirect proxy.

Key finding

Useful signals, with clear limits.

Suspended solids and turbidity showed stronger relationships with Sentinel-2 reflectance than pH. Some particle-associated metals could be modelled indirectly, but heavy metals cannot be identified directly from multispectral satellite bands.

Field sampling remains essential: the satellite-derived maps complement measured evidence rather than replace it.
